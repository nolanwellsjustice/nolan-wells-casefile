Plain-language overview · July 3–6, 2026

What happened to Nolan Wells?

Nolan Wells, 18, was reported missing after a gathering at Horn Island, Mississippi, on July 4, 2026. His body was recovered near the island’s northwestern end on the morning of July 6, 2026. This summary explains what the available public evidence supports—and what it does not establish.

Published July 28, 2026 · last reviewed August 28, 2026

Short answer

The public record establishes the trip, several vessel movements, the search, and the recovery. It does not establish what happened to Nolan during the final unresolved period on Horn Island.

The narrowest defensible reconstruction places the critical gap between approximately 3:45 PM and 4:31 PM on July 4, 2026. The precise order of the altercation, circulated video, witness departures, alleged boarding exchange, and Nolan’s last known movements has not been independently authenticated minute by minute. A reproduced phone record places the Sea Tow call later, at 4:48 PM.

Witness and media sequence

What is reported, but not independently timed to the minute

  1. Before 11:14 AM: Tracestin Shepherd says Nolan called for help locating where to anchor. The approximate time is based on his public recollection.
  2. Early afternoon: Nolan reportedly swims and socializes among the boats. A circulated photograph supports his presence but does not supply an authenticated capture time.
  3. Around 3:00 PM: Warren says this was about the last time he personally saw Nolan. This is a witness estimate, not an official timestamp.
  4. Around 3:30 PM: Tracestin says he was involved in an altercation and was forced back aboard his family’s separate boat.
  5. Around 4:01 PM, reported: The circulated Horn Island video records Tracestin yelling. The original device file and native metadata are not public.
  6. Around 4:01 PM, witness interpretation: Ivy Elizabeth says the clip followed the fight and that someone in the background only resembled Nolan; she expressly says the identification was not certain.
  7. Before 4:31 PM: Public witness accounts say Nolan remained on or near the island and did not leave with one group. The exact words, time, and complete listener set remain unresolved.
  8. 4:31–4:48 PM: Official movement is underway, but the exact tow-line attachment, emergency-discovery sequence, and vessel configuration are not public.
  9. 4:48 PM, displayed record: A released distress call reports bilge-pump failure, water ingress, approximately seven people aboard, and 30°14.684′ N, 88°46.790′ W as the vessel’s current coordinate. The caller says the vessel is not aground and that private help has been arranged.
  10. 4:31–5:24 PM: Official GPS verifies 2.75 miles of slow movement. The distress-call sequence makes private assistance or towing the strongest explanation, but GPS alone does not prove the tow mechanics.

Search and recovery

July 4–6, 2026

Displayed records show a five-minute call to Nolan’s mother at 11:07 PM, a 21-minute outgoing Coast Guard call at 11:13 PM, messages beginning at 11:15 PM, and another family call at 11:35 PM. MDMR opened a missing-person CAD call at 11:45:14 PM. At 1:38 AM on July 5, MDMR recorded an early working explanation that Nolan had returned on another vessel; that entry establishes the assessment, not that the reported ride occurred. Private searchers reached Horn Island before or alongside MDMR’s approximately 8:00 AM launch, followed by drone, Coast Guard, sonar, and modeling work through the night. Authorities reportedly received notice around 8:45 AM on July 6 that an NPS ranger had found Nolan near the northwest tip. That is a notification time and may not be the exact discovery minute.
